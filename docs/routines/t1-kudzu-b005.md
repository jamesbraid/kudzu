# Kernel routine role statements, IP30 and IP35 kernels

Each entry states what one kernel routine appears to do, drawn only from its instruction facts: registers, loads and stores, call sites with their arguments, and branch conditions. The routines are in the 6.5.22 IP30 and IP35 kernel images, and the addresses are the ones in those images. Callee names come from the image's symbol table, so a name hints at intent without proving it. The facts do not establish what any of these routines is for, so every entry is labelled inferred. No entry in this batch was dropped.

## IP30 interrupt and error paths

- `ecc_handler` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa80000002001b9d4
  **Inferred.** Reads an error record through a1 at offsets +0x18, +0x28 to +0x34 and +0x238, and writes status back into it and into a0 at +0x3b8. It runs a series of `ecc_cacheOp` (0xa80000002001b3b8) calls with operation codes 0x0 to 0x13, and `ecc_force_cache_line_invalid` (0xa80000002001b8dc) at three call sites. It formats a value with `cer_to_hex` (0xa80000002001b930) and a DIMM name from `maddr_to_dimm` (0xa80000002001202c), printed through `sprintf` (0xa8000000201a7100). The codes passed to `ecc_cacheOp` are not named by the facts, so which cache lines it touches is open.
- `VEC_ibe` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa80000002001f970
  **Inferred.** Loads from the absolute address 0xffffffffaff50000 and stores a doubleword at a0+0x140 after reading a halfword at 0xffffffffffffc3a0. It then jumps to `VEC_trap` (0xa80000002000f3d0), passing a0, a1 and a3 through. The name suggests an instruction bus error vector, but the facts show only the handoff and not how the error is decoded.
- `heart_cpu_intr` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020034998
  **Inferred.** Reads the halfword at 0xffffffffffffc3a0, stores a doubleword to 0xffffffffaff10028, and calls `doacvec` (0xa8000000201b2eac) with its four entry arguments. It then calls `doactions` (0xa8000000201b2bf0) and returns that call's value. The register at 0xffffffffaff10028 is the same one `clear_cpu_intr` and `heart_debug_intr` write, but the facts do not say what the register is.
- `heart_debug_intr` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa8000000200349d0
  **Inferred.** Reads the same halfword at 0xffffffffffffc3a0, stores a doubleword to 0xffffffffaff10028, and calls `debug` (0xa8000000200103dc) with a0 set to the constant 0xa800000020340160 and a1 to a3 passed through. It returns `debug`'s value. Reading it as a debugger interrupt comes from the callee name, since the facts do not show what `debug` does with its arguments.
- `chkdebug` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020034a08
  **Inferred.** Reads the same halfword, loads a doubleword from 0xffffffffaff10030 and stores to 0xffffffffaff10028. It tests a2 against the loaded value (branch at 0xa800000020034a28) and calls `debug` (0xa8000000200103dc) only when a bit is set, then returns. The bits involved are not named by the facts.
- `clear_cpu_intr` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020034a4c
  **Inferred.** Stores one doubleword to 0xffffffffaff10028 and returns the constant 0xffffffffaff10000, the base of the same register block. The stored value does not appear in the facts, so the "clear" reading comes from the name and the store target alone.
- `heart_mlreset` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020034a64
  **Inferred.** Calls `getcpuid` (0xa8000000200050b4) and then fills the register block at 0xffffffffaff10000: doublewords go to offsets +0x0, +0x8, +0x10, +0x18 and +0x40, and a doubleword is stored through table(0xffffffffaff10000). It also writes to the 0x9000000018000044, 0x9000000018000060 and 0x9000000018000068 addresses. The reading suggests a per-processor reset of software shadow state, but the stored values are not decoded, so the reset's scope is partly open.
- `heart_startup` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020034b78
  **Inferred.** Registers a crosstalk provider and its widgets in the hardware graph. It calls `hwgraph_path_add` (0xa8000000202201cc) four times, then `xtalk_provider_register` (0xa800000020030c78), `xtalk_provider_startup` (0xa8000000200308ec), `xswitch_id_set` (0xa800000020031304), `hwgraph_edge_add` (0xa80000002021f9d8), `device_master_set` (0xa8000000202217d0) and `xwidget_register` (0xa800000020030d8c) twice. `xswitch_info_get` (0xa800000020031098) and `xswitch_info_link_ok` (0xa80000002003129c) decide whether each widget's work runs, inside a loop whose counter is tested at 0xa800000020034d2c. What the widgets are comes from the callee names.
- `heart_intr` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020035bcc
  **Inferred.** Loads an 8-byte mask from 0xffffffffaff10038 and stores to 0xffffffffaff10028. It calls `cvsema` (0xa800000020137f28) once, then loops on `ffintr64` (0xa800000020005300), which returns a bit index. An index of 0x3b or more branches to `buserror_intr` (0xa800000020154f08) at 0xa800000020035c6c; a lower index signals a per-source entry in the table at 0xa800000020398218 through `valusema` (0xa8000000201384b0) and `vsema` (0xa800000020138280). The semaphore meanings come from the callee names.
- `heart_rev` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020035ef4
  **Inferred.** Returns the signed word cached at 0xa8000000203db3c4. When that word is negative (branch at 0xa800000020035ef8), the routine reads the word at 0x9000000018000004 and returns that instead, which suggests the cache is empty until first read. The facts do not say what the revision bits mean.

## Crosstalk bridges and their register accessors

- `giobr_attach` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa8000000200367f4
  **Inferred.** Reads the word at 0x900000001800002c and tests it against 0x80 at 0xa80000002003680c. It maps the bridge window with `xtalk_piotrans_addr` (0xa800000020030504), allocates a 0x108-byte block with `kmem_alloc` (0xa800000020130644) and clears it with `blkclr` (0xa80000002000affc). It runs `do_giobr_hw_init` (0xa80000002003666c), probes slots with `giobr_slot_probe` (0xa800000020036474) under a counter tested against 4, then resets the link with `do_giobr_link_reset` (0xa800000020037d04) and registers the GIO provider and its devices. The `cmn_err` call at 0xa800000020036ac8 is the only error report among its calls, and the return value is -1 or 0.
- `xbridge_attach` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020037e18
  **Inferred.** Gets the bridge pointer from `pcibr_bridge_ptr_get` (0xa80000002003c8ec) and reads identity, status, speed and mode through `pcireg_id_get` (0xa800000020046398), `pcireg_stat_get` (0xa8000000200464f8), `pcireg_speed_get` (0xa800000020046ff4) and `pcireg_mode_get` (0xa80000002004716c). It allocates a 0xab8-byte state block with `kmem_zalloc` (0xa800000020130768) and registers the PCI I/O provider with `pciio_provider_register` (0xa800000020055c08). It then sets interrupt routing through the `pcireg_intr_*` setters and `xtalk_intr_connect` (0xa8000000200307b4), builds an address translation pool with `rmallocmap` (0xa80000002015071c) and `pcibr_init_ext_ate_ram` (0xa80000002003ce2c), and maps PCI windows with `pciio_device_win_map_new` (0xa80000002005610c). The register bits behind these calls are not decoded, so the window layout is only as named in the calls.
- `pcireg_id_get` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020046398
  **Inferred.** Panics through `panic` (0xa8000000201a749c) when a0 is null. It reads a halfword at +0x72, a word at +0x4 and a doubleword at +0x8 from a0, and compares the halfword against small codes (0, 1, 2, 3 and -1) and then a chain of 16-bit codes from 0xe000 to 0xc002. Two words are checked against 0x24 and 0x36 on the way out. The exits return 0 or an unnamed value, and the name points to an ID register that the facts do not confirm.
- `pcireg_stat_get` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa8000000200464f8
  **Inferred.** Has the same shape as `pcireg_id_get`: a null check that panics, a halfword at +0x72, a word at +0x4, a doubleword at +0x8, and the same family of comparisons. The panic message differs from its sibling's. The name suggests a status register, but the facts do not establish which register it reads.
- `pcireg_nic_addr` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa800000020049f30
  **Inferred.** Uses the same accessor shape: a null check that panics, a halfword at +0x72, a word at +0x4 and a doubleword at +0x8. Its failure path calls `panic` (0xa8000000201a749c) through a different message pointer. The name and its use in `xbridge_attach` point to the address of a NIC part on the bridge, but nothing in these facts confirms that.
- `pcireg_intr_addr_vect_set` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa80000002004cd7c
  **Inferred.** Reads a doubleword at a0+0x8, a halfword at +0x72 and a word at +0x4, and stores through pointers the facts do not resolve. Its assertion (`assfail`, 0xa80000002000f694) passes source line 0x85b, and its panic call takes a1 shifted left by 3 as an argument, which suggests an index into 8-byte entries. The register it writes is open, and the index range test is not among the facts traced here.
- `pcireg_intr_addr_addr_set` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa80000002004d1e8
  **Inferred.** Has the same structure as `pcireg_intr_addr_vect_set`: the same field reads, the same kind of unresolved stores, and an assertion (`assfail`, 0xa80000002000f694) that passes source line 0x89a. It appears to set the address half of the interrupt slot that the sibling routine sets the vector for, but the facts do not show the register.

## Keyboard, console and Ethernet

- `pckm_attach` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa80000002005772c
  **Inferred.** Attaches a keyboard and mouse controller under the IOC3 chip. It checks `ioc3_subdev_enabled` (0xa80000002005756c), allocates blocks of 0x68 and 0x58 bytes with `kmem_zalloc`, and creates keyboard and mouse nodes through `hwgraph_char_device_add` (0xa80000002022079c) with `hwgraph_chmod` set to 0x1b6. It connects an interrupt with `ioc3_intr_connect` (0xa800000020056b14), and sends the bytes 0xf5, 0xf4 and 0xf6 through `pckm_pollcmd` (0xa800000020058ee0), checking each reply against 0xfa with `data_check` (0xa800000020059340). The byte values are shown by the facts, but their meaning as keyboard commands is not.
- `cn_init` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa80000002005f8a8
  **Inferred.** Initializes a console state block at 0xa8000000203fb950, calling `gfx_earlyinit` (0xa80000002031f924) first. It reads a byte at `arg_console` (0xa800000020437bd8) and compares it with 0x67 and 0x47 at 0xa80000002005f970 and 0xa80000002005f978. It then calls `is_specified` (0xa8000000201b69a0), `tp_init` (0xa80000002031f6bc) and `du_init` (0xa8000000201301f8), and sets up a spinlock and a mutex in the block. The facts do not show which console path the two byte values select.
- `ef_ioc3eaddr` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa8000000200ade4c
  **Inferred.** Loads a pointer from a0+0x398 and a word from +0x38c. It passes address fields from that structure to `nic_get_eaddr` (0xa80000002002fbf8), and reports a failure through `cmn_err` (0xa8000000201a63b4) at level 1. The return value is 0 or 5, and the facts do not say whether 5 is an error number.
- `doacvec` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa8000000201b2eac
  **Inferred.** Loads bytes at 0xffffffffffffc080 and 0xffffffffffffc081, a word at c084 and a doubleword at c0a0, and stores to c04e, c081 and c0a0. Depending on those values it calls `panicspin` (0xa8000000201a846c), `tlbflush_rand` (0xa800000020192670) with 1, or `tlbflush` (0xa80000002019257c) with a0 taken from a global at -0x3f60. The one caller in these facts is `heart_cpu_intr`, which passes its entry arguments through, so its role on the interrupt path is open.

## Process timers and the syssgi call

- `dogetitimer` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa8000000201f59bc
  **Inferred.** Writes four doublewords to a1 at +0x0, +0x8, +0x10 and +0x18, reading their values through a pointer chain that starts at a2+752. It takes a mutex at [a2+752]+2144 with `mutex_lock` (0xa80000002013b39c), calls `chktimeout_tick` (0xa8000000200093c4) for the remaining count, and converts the result with `tick_to_timeval` (0xa8000000201f6b4c). The name suggests `getitimer`, but the facts show only the timeval conversion, not an interval timer layout.
- `syssgi` in `6.5.22/unix.IP30` at 0xa8000000201cf9c4
  **Inferred.** Is the system call entry for the `syssgi` family. It starts by calling `get_current_abi` (0xa80000002033aa04), copies request blocks in with many `copyin` calls (0xa80000002000ac80), and compares the doubleword at a0 against 0x4e3 at 0xa8000000201cfa14. It dispatches to many helpers, including job control (`syssgi_makenewjob` 0xa800000020339388, `syssgi_joinjob` 0xa8000000203393a4, `syssgi_killjob` 0xa80000002033936c), `kaio_rw` (0xa8000000203394fc) and `xfs_itable` (0xa800000020282860), and unmatched requests go to `nosys` (0xa800000020154e74). The facts do not show every compare that picks a helper, so the request-to-helper mapping rests partly on callee names.

## IP35 restart and node id

- `restart` in `6.5.22/unix.IP35` at 0xc000000000041010
  **Inferred.** The restart path for the IP35 platform. It calls `us_delay` (0xc000000000069c48) three times, reads the system controller through `get_elsc` (0xc000000000065554), and powers down with `sc_power_down_all` (0xc0000000000d24f8). It has a backward branch at 0xc0000000000410f0 to 0xc0000000000410c0, and it resets network ports with `ni_reset_port` (0xc000000000041304) further on, at 0xc000000000041108. It gets the node id from `get_nasid` (0xc000000000041204), runs `elsc_init` (0xc0000000000d0c40) and `elsc_system_reset` (0xc0000000000d212c), and prints through `printf` (0xc00000000026fdb4) on one path. It stores to 0x9200000001680008 and 0x9200000001680010 near the exit; the loop bounds and delay units are not decoded.
- `get_nasid` in `6.5.22/unix.IP35` at 0xc000000000041204
  **Inferred.** Loads a doubleword from the hub address 0x9200000001600000 and returns a value the facts leave unresolved. The name says it returns the node's NASID, but the facts contain no shift or mask that would pull that field out of the loaded word, so the field layout is open.
